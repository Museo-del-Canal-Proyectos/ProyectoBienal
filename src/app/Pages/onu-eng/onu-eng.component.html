Migration in Darien
data and humanitarian responses

The Darién jungle crossing is one of the most dangerous migration routes in the world. In 2023, more than 520,000 people undertook this journey, including more than 113,000 children.

The lack of infrastructure, exposure to violence, and extreme poverty make this a high-risk journey. Organizations such as UNICEF, UNHCR, and other humanitarian agencies worked in the region to provide lifesaving assistance: access to clean water, medical care, psychosocial support, and protection for the most vulnerable populations, especially women, children, and adolescents. Through data collection and program implementation in the region, these organizations sought not only to respond to the immediate crisis but also to raise awareness about the factors driving migration and the urgent needs of those transiting through Darién.

ACNUR

In Darién, UNHCR supports the Government of Panama’s response at the Temporary Reception Stations (ETRMs) and in host communities by providing safe spaces and protection from harsh weather conditions, as well as access to basic health and mental health services. UNHCR also provides information about the risks of the journey

UNHCR offers legal support to individuals with international protection needs, referring those who require specialized assistance to the appropriate institutions. It also provides information about the asylum system in Panama and assists those in need with their refugee status applications. Additionally, UNHCR supports Indigenous communities in Darién by facilitating workshops and programs aimed at strengthening and promoting social cohesion and peaceful coexistence.

UNFPA

The United Nations Population Fund (UNFPA) in Panama addresses the humanitarian crisis in Darién through a comprehensive approach that includes human development, humanitarian action, and peacebuilding, with a focus on sexual and reproductive health and the prevention of gender-based violence. Through this approach, more than 26,000 women received reproductive health services, and over 33,000 benefited from information on gender-based violence prevention.

In addition, in collaboration with the Ministry of Health, UNFPA inaugurated the Metetí Maternity Home to improve maternal health services and reduce mortality. It also established the first Comprehensive Care Center for survivors of violence in Metetí. Between 2023 and 2024, the project operated with a budget of 568,000 USD and benefited more than 59,000 women and girls, significantly contributing to saving lives.

OIM

The International Organization for Migration (IOM), in coordination with United Nations system partners and civil society, contributes to the efforts of the Panamanian government in responding to mixed movements entering through the Darién jungle. IOM provides life-saving humanitarian assistance to vulnerable migrants in transit through the distribution of food and essential non-food items (NFIs), the provision of health services—including psychosocial and mental health support—and the delivery of medicines and medical supplies, as well as protection services.

IOM also supports the improvement and rehabilitation of Temporary Reception Stations (ETRMs), facilitates capacity-building activities in Camp Coordination and Camp Management (CCCM), and co-leads and supports interagency and interinstitutional coordination mechanisms on migration at various levels.

UNICEF

UNICEF works in some of the world’s most challenging places to reach the most disadvantaged children and protect the rights of every child, everywhere. In over 190 countries and territories, we do whatever it takes to help every child survive, thrive, and fulfill their potential—from early childhood through adolescence. UNICEF is on the ground before, during, and after humanitarian emergencies, bringing hope and life-saving assistance to children and their families.

In Panama, we have been present since 1991 with our own office, and over more than three decades of work for children in the country, we have contributed to building water and sanitation systems in rural communities; supported the enactment of legislation that protects children from abuse, harassment, child labor, trafficking, and sexual exploitation; provided technical assistance to strengthen institutions such as MIDES, SENNIAF, MINSA, and MEDUCA; and developed awareness campaigns to prevent child abuse and promote nurturing parenting, among other key initiatives. We are apolitical and impartial—we are never neutral when it comes to defending children’s rights and safeguarding their lives and futures.

ONU MUJERES

UN Women’s Trayectos project focuses on empowering women affected by the human mobility crisis in Central America, where an unprecedented flow of people is driven by conflict, climate change, and other factors. Over the past fifteen years, the number of people on the move has doubled, with 48.7% being women and girls, who face risks such as gender-based violence, discrimination, and human trafficking. With support from the Government of Japan, the project is being implemented in Costa Rica, Honduras, and Panama between March 2024 and February 2025. One of the main challenges is the violence women face along their journey, including systemic sexual violence and extortion. The goal is for these women to participate in social cohesion initiatives and benefit from gender protection services. Through the program, UN Women works to strengthen women’s leadership and ensure equal access in this context of mixed migration, aligning with global commitments such as the Women, Peace and Security agenda. The project is expected to enhance the capacity of humanitarian actors to provide gender protection services, increase access to such services for women, girls, and LGBTIQ+ individuals, and strengthen partnerships with women’s groups and United Nations agencies. UN Women is committed to protecting and empowering women and girls in situations of humanitarian crisis and human mobility, ensuring responses that address their specific needs while promoting their leadership and access to comprehensive protection services.

CRUZ ROJA PANAMEÑA

The Panamanian Red Cross, founded in 1917, is a humanitarian organization that serves as an auxiliary to public institutions, with over a century of service to the country. It is part of the International Red Cross and Red Crescent Movement, and its work is grounded in alleviating human suffering and saving lives.

Throughout its history, it has provided aid during emergencies, crises, and disasters, as well as assistance to vulnerable communities and support for migrants after their journey through the Darién jungle, offering humanitarian services without distinction of race, religion, social status, or ideology. Its emblem represents help, protection, and solidarity in the most difficult times.
